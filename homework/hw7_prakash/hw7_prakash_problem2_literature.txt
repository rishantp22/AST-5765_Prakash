Literature overview: HD 189733b


Paper 1
Knutson et al. (2007)
"A map of the day-night contrast of the extrasolar planet HD 189733b"

Knutson et al. studied the hot Jupiter HD 189733b to understand how efficiently its atmosphere redistributes heat from the permanently illuminated dayside to the
nightside. Using the 8 μm channel of the Spitzer Space Telescope, they monitored the system for more than half an orbit and measured changes in infrared brightness as
different parts of the planet rotated into view. They found that the planet’s hemisphere-averaged brightness temperature varied from about 973 K to 1212 K, indicating a
relatively small day-night temperature contrast and therefore efficient heat transport through the atmosphere. The brightest region was also shifted eastward from the
substellar point, showing that atmospheric winds move the hottest region away from the point receiving the most direct stellar radiation. Overall, the study showed that infrared
phase curves can be used to infer large-scale atmospheric circulation and temperature structure on exoplanets that cannot be directly resolved.

Paper 2
Charbonneau et al. (2008)
"The Broadband Infrared Emission Spectrum of the Exoplanet HD 189733b"

Charbonneau et al. used secondary-eclipse observations from the Spitzer Space Telescope to measure the dayside infrared emission of the hot Jupiter HD 189733b at
wavelengths from 3.6 to 24 μm. By comparing the planet-to-star flux ratios across several infrared bands, they found that the planet’s spectrum is not consistent with
simple blackbody radiation. The unusually strong emission near 3.6–4 μm is interpreted as radiation escaping through a region of relatively low atmospheric opacity, while the
lower emission between about 4.5 and 8 μm is consistent with molecular absorption, particularly from water vapor. Their atmospheric models favor a temperature structure
without a high-altitude thermal inversion, unlike the more strongly irradiated hot Jupiter HD 209458b. Overall, the study showed that broadband secondary-eclipse
measurements can reveal the temperature structure, molecular absorption, and energy transport within exoplanet atmospheres.


Paper 3
Evans et al. (2013)
"The Deep Blue Color of HD 189733b"

Evans et al. used Hubble Space Telescope observations of the secondary eclipse of HD 189733b to measure how much visible light the planet reflects and therefore determine
its color and atmospheric properties. They found that the geometric albedo is relatively high at blue wavelengths, about 0.40 between 290 and 450 nm, but drops to below 0.12
between 450 and 570 nm, indicating that the planet would appear deep blue. The authors interpret this strong wavelength dependence as evidence for reflective clouds
or haze that efficiently scatter short-wavelength light, while absorption, particularly from sodium at longer visible wavelengths, reduces the reflected signal toward the red. Their
simple atmospheric models suggest that clouds located at intermediate altitudes can reproduce the observed albedo trend, showing that visible-light secondary eclipses can
provide constraints on the scattering particles, absorbers, and vertical cloud structure of hot-Jupiter atmospheres.


Paper 4
Zhang et al. (2024)
"Retrievals on NIRCam transmission and emission spectra of HD 189733b with PLATON 6"

Zhang et al. used JWST/NIRCam transmission and emission spectra of the hot Jupiter HD 189733b to better constrain its atmospheric composition and thermal structure.
Using an updated version of the PLATON retrieval code, they found that the atmosphere has a metallicity of several times the solar value and a C/O ratio of about 0.4, with
broadly consistent results from both transit and eclipse observations. The spectra show strong evidence for water, carbon dioxide, carbon monoxide, and hydrogen sulfide,
while methane appears depleted at the terminator, possibly because of photochemistry or atmospheric mixing. The emission data also provide tentative evidence for a weak
thermal inversion at low pressures on the dayside. In addition to these atmospheric results, the paper introduces PLATON 6, which includes GPU acceleration and improved
opacity and retrieval capabilities designed for JWST-quality observations. Overall, the study demonstrates how JWST can provide much tighter constraints on exoplanet
atmospheres while also showing that some inferred properties, especially C/O ratio, remain sensitive to model assumptions.
